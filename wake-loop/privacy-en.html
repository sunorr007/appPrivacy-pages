Privacy Policy — Wake Loop

Last updated: 25 November 2026 · Applies to the Android app Wake Loop (package com.bjfhkj.wakeup)

Overview. Wake Loop is a local-first alarm application. It has no account system, requires no registration and operates no server of ours. Alarms, stickers and wake history remain on the user's device. Camera frames are processed in memory only and are never stored or uploaded. The only third parties involved are Google advertising and analytics services, which start only after this policy has been accepted.

1. What the app does with your data

Everything below happens on your device. Nothing is sent to us, because we do not operate a backend.

  • Alarms and schedules — stored in a local database on your phone.
  • Wake records and statistics — stored locally; used to draw your streaks, heatmap and reports.
  • Registered stickers — the code value you scanned is stored locally so the alarm can recognise it again.
  • Camera frames — used in memory to scan a sticker or compare a reference photo. Frames are discarded immediately after recognition. We never store them, never upload them, and never write them to your gallery.
  • Motion and step sensors — read only while a step or shake task is running, then stopped. Not written to Google Fit or any health platform.
  • Backup code printing — the image is handed to the Android print or share sheet you choose. We do not keep a copy.
  • Preferences — ring theme, shake strength, ad personalisation and similar settings, stored locally.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of the above. The app has no account system and we retain no copy of the data, so no separate account deletion is required.

2. Advertising

Wake Loop shows a small number of ads through Google AdMob. To select and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may access:

  • your device's advertising ID (a resettable identifier);
  • device model, operating-system version and language;
  • IP address and coarse network information;
  • app-install attribution data through Google Play.

Google processes this data under its own privacy policy: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

How to control it

  • In the app: Me → Settings → Personalized ads. Turning it off makes the next ad request non-personalised.
  • At the OS level: reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Google → Ads.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland: a certified consent dialog (Google UMP) appears on first launch. You can change or withdraw that choice at any time from Settings → Ad privacy options.

We do not show ads on the alarm ring screen or on any task screen. Ads are capped at a few native cards per day and never repeat the same creative across pages within a short window.

3. Analytics

The international build is prepared to use Firebase Analytics for aggregate usage statistics. In the current release it is not active: no Google services configuration is bundled, so no analytics events leave the device. Firebase collection is additionally disabled at the manifest level until you grant consent at runtime. If a future release activates it, this section and the in-app third-party SDK list will be updated first.

4. Legal bases (GDPR / UK GDPR)

  • Consent — for advertising identifiers and personalised ads. You may withdraw it at any time in Settings.
  • Legitimate interests — keeping the app working, preventing ad abuse and measuring aggregate install quality, balanced against your rights.
  • Contract — none; there is no paid service and no account.

5. California and other US state privacy rights

We do not sell personal information for money. Sharing an advertising identifier with Google for personalised advertising may be considered "sharing" or "cross-context behavioural advertising" under the CPRA and similar state laws. You can opt out:

  • turn off Personalized ads in Settings — this applies to the next ad request;
  • reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings;
  • email support@fenghongtech.cn and we will confirm what, if anything, applies to you.

We do not knowingly collect sensitive personal information, we do not use data for profiling that produces legal effects, and we do not have actual knowledge of any sale or sharing of the personal information of minors.

6. Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to processing of your personal data, and to data portability. Because the app stores everything locally and we hold no server-side records, the practical answers are:

  • Access / portability — everything the app knows is visible inside the app on your device.
  • Deletion — uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android Settings.
  • Objection / withdrawal of consent — Settings → Personalized ads, or Settings → Ad privacy options.
  • Complaints — you may contact your local data-protection authority. We will respond to any request sent to support@fenghongtech.cn within 30 days.

7. International transfers

We do not transfer data ourselves. Google may process advertising data in countries outside your own; Google relies on Standard Contractual Clauses and its own safeguards for those transfers, described in its privacy policy linked above.

8. Children's personal information

Wake Loop is intended for users of all ages and sets no age limit. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and we do not use children's personal information for advertising profiles or disclose it to third parties. If you believe a child has used the app, uninstall it; we hold no copy of the data to delete.

9. Security

All network requests made by the app (advertising, remote ad-frequency policy) use HTTPS. Data at rest is protected by the Android app sandbox. We keep no copy of your data on any server; even in the event of a breach of our systems, your alarms, photos or wake history would not be exposed.

10. Changes to this policy

If a change affects what we collect, we will update this page and the in-app privacy sheet before the change ships, and where required ask for your consent again. The "last updated" date above always reflects the current version.
